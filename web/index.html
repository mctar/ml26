Moonlander, Roadster edition

Jack Burness wrote Moonlander in 1973 for DEC's GT40, a PDP-11 with a vector display and a light pen. This is that program: recovered, translated routine by routine into Rust, proved against the original binary step for step, and compiled to run here. The physics, the rules, the terrain, the messages and the fuel budget are his, unchanged. What you see is 2026.

The car

A Roadster, the one-seat kind that is meant to leave the ground, coming down on compressed air. It weighs 10,086 lb because that is the number the 1973 code divides by, and that number is an address, not a weight. Nobody has published a weight for the real one either.

The ground

The terrain is Burness's table at true scale, which makes it a range of needles up to 13,282 feet tall. The program measures your height against that table until you are below 450 feet; then it switches to its close-up and treats the ground as a quarter of its height, and the 1973 display cut to a new picture at that moment so that nobody noticed. This version keeps both heights and cuts the camera in the same place. The mountain the readout says you are 10,000 feet above is the mountain you hit.

Controls

Drag the thrust bar, or use the up and down keys. The arrows turn; Shift makes them the big arrows. Under the hood every one of these moves a light pen onto the controls Burness drew, and the program reads nothing but the pen. V shows the 1973 tube. R starts again. S copies a link that replays this flight exactly. H opens the guide, with the rest.

The voice

The messages are the program's, read over the radio by a synthetic controller: a small text-to-speech model run once, then a 300 to 3400 Hz band, a compressor and a little grit, with the squelch and static added live. The driver is the program's man: he climbs out when it shows him, walks where it walks him, and climbs back in before it lifts the car.

How to fly

Put the Roadster down on Mare Fremont slowly enough to walk away from it: falling at under 8 ft/s, nearly upright, hardly moving sideways, on level ground. Land near the diner and the driver goes in for an order. Land anywhere else and he plants a flag.

The flight is paused while this page is open.

The thrusters push the way the floor of the car faces. Upright, they slow your fall; tilted, they also push you sideways. Thrust runs from 10 to 100%. Somewhere between a quarter and a half holds the car steady, but the program’s arithmetic moves that point as you go, so fly by the readouts rather than the bar.

Touchdown

The program judges a landing by how fast you were falling when you touched, and says so over the radio.

08153060 ft/s
  • Under 8WELL, IT WORKED.
  • 8 to 15CONGRATULATIONS ON A GOOD LANDING
  • 15 to 30THE LANDING WAS A LITTLE FAST
  • 30 to 60THE LANDING WAS TOO FAST AND DAMAGE WAS DONE TO THE SHIP
  • 60 or moreWELL, YOU CERTAINLY BLEW THAT ONE. THERE WERE NO SURVIRORS

Whatever the speed, the car tips over if it comes down moving sideways at more than 10 ft/s, leaning more than 15°, or across a step of 48 ft or more in the ground. The thrusters cut out by themselves 3 ft from the ground.

On the way down

  • The radio warns you when you are falling faster than 15, 30 and 60 ft/s.
  • Boulders: pass within 15 ft above one and you hit it.
  • Cars left by earlier landings: within 26 ft above a parked one, or 17 ft above a wreck, and you hit it.
  • Flags from earlier landings: thrust within 26 ft of one and you blow it away.
  • The diner: come within 30 ft above it and there is no more diner.
  • Leave by either edge, or climb above 25,000 ft, and you are put back with no air at all.

After you land

If the diner is on the close-up when you touch down, the driver climbs out, walks to the window, orders TWO CHEESEBURGERS AND A BIG MAC TO GO., carries the bag back and climbs in, and the car lifts off with a little more air. Anywhere else he plants a flag.

However a flight ends, a card sums it up. Play again keeps the same Moon, and the Moon remembers: your flags, parked cars, wrecks and craters are there on the next descent, waiting to be flown into. New Moon starts clean, with the diner back if you knocked it down.

Keys

V switches between the Roadster and the 1973 tube, where the mouse is the light pen: hold it on the bar for thrust, on the arrows to turn. R starts again. S copies a link that replays this flight. A hands the controls to the autopilot and back. M turns the sound off and on. H opens this guide and Esc closes it.
